About

Give me a messy dataset and a decision someone needs to make, and I will get you from one to the other. I work across healthcare, energy and industrial domains, mostly on the kind of data that arrives incomplete, imbalanced, and split across sources that do not agree with each other.

Recent work includes survival and risk models on a 140,000-patient clinical dataset, real-time analytics across 100+ industrial sensors, a retrieval-augmented generation system over an 800-document archive, and a bio-signal study where I collected the data myself. I build in Python, R and SQL with scikit-learn, TensorFlow, pandas and gradient boosting, and deploy on Azure and AWS.

MSc in Artificial Intelligence, Distinction, University of Essex. I have also taught programming and statistics to 100+ students, which is where I learned that a model nobody understands is a model nobody uses.

How I got here

I was not a good student. For years I did the minimum and scraped through, and I had quietly accepted that this was just who I was.

That changed when I stopped treating studying as something done to me and started treating it as something I was actually trying to get good at. Not a burst of motivation, just a different daily habit: work the problem, find where my understanding breaks, fix that, repeat.

The results took a while to show up, and then they did. I finished my BSc in Software Engineering as a gold medalist, first in the School of Information Technology. I went on to an MSc in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Essex with Distinction, and won the MSc Project Prize for the highest dissertation marks across the department's MSc degrees. In the cohort I worked alongside, I finished in the top 10%.

I mention this not because the medals matter on their own, but because of what the gap between the two versions taught me. I know exactly what it looks like when someone is stuck and cannot see a way through, because I was that person for years. It is why I care so much that the people I hand a model to can actually understand it, and why I spent years teaching programming and statistics to students who thought they were bad at both.

The same instinct shows up in my work. Difficult data does not put me off. It is usually the sign that there is something worth finding.

Survival modelling on 140,000 patients

Edgec.io · 2025 to 2026

Scraping and OCR across 100+ sites

Built scraping pipelines with BeautifulSoup and Playwright across 100+ structurally different websites, cutting collection time by roughly 40%. University of Essex · 2023 to 2024 · MSc Project Prize

Predicting emotion from four synchronised bio-signals

A study I ran end to end rather than a dataset handed to me. I collected the data in the lab: skin conductance and heart rate from Shimmer electrodes, pupil size from a Tobii Pro eye tracker, and facial expression from video, all synchronised in iMotions across 800+ participant-stimulus recordings. From there I engineered statistical descriptors per signal, built ground truth from self-report surveys using multidimensional scaling, and compared five regression models under leave-one-out cross-validation in Python with scikit-learn and TensorFlow.

The part I am proudest of is not the model. I wrote automated signal-quality checks that flagged dropout in the facial-expression stream, which exposed recordings that were up to 93% missing and traced the cause to lighting and camera placement. That finding changed the data collection protocol for the work that followed. I also shipped a configurable command-line tool so the next student could regenerate every feature from raw exports without reading my code. The dissertation was awarded the MSc Project Prize for the highest marks in the department.
